# OJZ act 1: the lag he still feels, and the fall that feels capped (2026-09-27)

Two owner reports on `s4.debug.bin` crc `8e6de1fa` (canonical OJZ act 1, DEBUG), the ROM his
player had open:
1. *"I feel like I still feel it"*: lag, while PLAYING the act in real physics, after the
   soft-budget fix `ff7cbad5` landed.
2. *"the capped fall speed kind of feels bad too"*.

Branch `perf/ojz-feel`, based on `origin/master` `aa636c0d` (after `ff7cbad5`). The base DEBUG
build reproduces the owner's ROM exactly: **`8e6de1fa`, 856,446 bytes**. Tools and raw results:
`docs/research/2026-09-27-ojz-feel/`.

**No engine code changes in this parcel.** Every fill-policy change tried (below) was either no
better or traded one leg for another, so none met the bar of a clear, correct change. Part 2
differs from S3K on purpose, by an owner ruling, so nothing was "fixed to match".

## The act, as a player meets it

`results/ojz_act1_collision.png` (`render_ojz.py`, from the editor tree) is the 3x3-section act
with its collision. **Only section 0 has collision authored in the form the bake reads**
(`section_0.collattr.bin` has 4,140 non-zero bytes; sections 1-8 have 0, with art and the old
`coll.bin` only). So playing the act means: section 0 (spawn, springs, the loop, a slab), then
running off either edge of section 0 into a **~5,300 px diagonal fall** through the
empty sections to the act floor (the camera clamp at y 5,920), then running on the act floor.
Every exit from section 0 is that fall. The canonical "run" leg of the earlier parcel ends
mid-fall (camera y 4,023) for the same reason.

## Part 1: the lag

### Legs (`legs.sh`, 16 legs, DEBUG, tick-keyed inputs)

`ojz_feel_probe.py` places the player through the DEBUG warp mailbox (after pressing B to leave
free flight), and keys every input to logic ticks, so a lag frame cannot move the path. Legs:
run right from spawn (with stuck-hops), spindash from spawn, the canonical jump rhythm, hold left
into section 0's springs, spindash off section 0's run-up and off its lower slab, reversing on
section 0's floor, three top-of-act falls holding right or left, a straight fall, a fall that
reverses mid-air, and the act floor run left, spindash both ways, jumps at speed and reversing.

### The lag map (base `8e6de1fa`, all 16 legs, 17,338 video frames)

| camera motion in the frame | lag / frames | rate |
|---|---|---|
| **diagonal** (both axes >= 4 px) | **54 / 2,825** | **1.91%** |
| vertical only | 2 / 858 | 0.23% |
| horizontal only | 7 / 5,628 | 0.12% |
| slow or still | 0 / 8,011 | 0 |

- **The lag is the diagonal fall.** Lag frames sit along the fall paths (camera buckets from
  x 1024-4096, y 1024-5120); section 0's own bucket holds 21 of them, all on the way off its
  edges at speed.
- **None** on the act floor (run, jumps at speed, spindash both ways, reversing), none in a
  straight fall, none holding left into the springs.
- Worst leg: **spindash off section 0's run-up, 25 lag in 575 frames**, one hitch every
  ~20 frames while he dives. Holding right off the ledge: 9 in 2,509.
- **Oscillation thrash (survey candidate 7): not present in OJZ.** Reversing every 60-90 ticks on
  section 0's floor and on the act floor, and reversing mid-fall: 0 lag on all three. The thrash
  the EHZ parcel saw needs the clip's spring dead end, which OJZ does not have.

### What makes each lag frame (`classify.py`, 60 lag windows over the 8 lagging legs)

Each lag window holds one overrunning tick (`lagwin.py` rule). Work per tick is the profiler's
sample minus `VSync_Wait`, including the lag frame's own VBlank (~13k).

| cause | lag windows | mean per tick |
|---|---|---|
| **decode burst**: the hard budget decodes 3-6 blocks at once | **37 of 60** | 5.6 decodes (3.4 real S4LZ at ~9.5k each), 1.4 columns, 153k |
| **column catch-up**: 4-9 column fills in one tick | 16 of 60 | 6.6 columns (~9.3k each), 3.0 decodes, 168k |
| steady over: an ordinary diagonal tick that tipped | 7 of 60 | 1.9 columns, 1.1 decodes, 140k |

**The diagonal baseline has no room for either burst.** A whole-fall segment profile (spindash
dive, 238 ticks, `sec0_loop_seg`) averages **102.6k cycles of work per tick** against the 127.8k
frame: the fill 36.1k (two rows 20.1k, columns 12.2k, decodes 3.2k), `RunObjects` 14.3k, VBlank
13.7k, `Section_UpdateColumns` 12.2k, `Parallax_Update` 10.6k, `Render_Sprites` 5.8k,
`Canopy_Probe` 4.0k, `PageCache_Audit` 2.9k. About 25k of headroom: three real decodes, or three
column copies, fill it.

**Why the bursts happen.** At a diagonal the fill needs about one decode per tick (a new row
strip every 8.5 ticks at 15 px/tick is ~6 blocks; a new column strip every 13 ticks at
9.8 px/tick is ~4-5). The soft budget allows one. A new block column needs 4-5 decodes, so it
completes over 4-5 ticks (longer when the pending-row resume, which runs first, takes the one
decode), and all horizontal work waits behind it: `--cache` shows `Cache_Fill_Resume_Col` held for 4-6 ticks while
the right lead falls 18 -> 12 (`sec0_loop_cache`). When any lead drops under 8 rows / 10 columns
the hard budget returns, decodes up to 6 blocks, and the unbounded column loop fills every column
the camera passed. That is the bang-bang of a soft/hard switch whose soft value is below the
diagonal's demand. Before `ff7cbad5` the fill ran the hard budget always and paid the same
bursts at different ticks.

### DEBUG-only cost, measured (he plays DEBUG)

Release vs DEBUG on the four spawn legs, same scripts, both settled on the spawn floor first
(`release_legs.sh`; paths agree except 32-41 ticks of mid-tick snapshot residue per leg):

| legs | release `f84c91a7` | DEBUG `8e6de1fa` | DEBUG with `Canopy_Probe` + `PageCache_Audit` stubbed |
|---|---|---|---|
| spawn right / spin / jump rhythm / springs | **6** | **18** | 13 |

On all 16 DEBUG legs, stubbing (`stub_rom.py`, an upper bound):

| | lag, 16 legs |
|---|---|
| DEBUG as built | 63 |
| `Canopy_Probe` stubbed | 56 |
| `PageCache_Audit` stubbed | 62 |
| both stubbed | 55 |

**Plainly: the DEBUG shape costs about two thirds of the lag on the spawn legs (18 vs 6).**
`Canopy_Probe` (4,026 cycles every tick) is worth about 7 of 63 frames over the whole leg set;
the amortised audit about 1. The rest of the DEBUG difference (18 -> 13 -> 6) is DEBUG code
that stubbing two routines cannot separate: asserts, the object-loop assert wrapper,
`Debug_TierTags_Update`, the demand-stall watchdog. `Canopy_Probe` stays armed; that is the
owner's call.

### Did `ff7cbad5` help or hurt here?

Same 16 legs on the parent `463dcfe5` (`pre`, crc `e90664c6`) and on base: **67 -> 63**. It
helped the jump rhythm (17 -> 9) and hurt the spindash dive (18 -> 25). Both runs on the same
emulator core (below).

### Fill-policy changes tried, and why none landed

All on the same leg set and the canonical fly legs (`fly_legs.sh`, DEBUG free flight, coverage).
Patch for the last variant: `results/experiment_graded_budget_column_cap.patch`.

| variant | 16 legs | fly diagonal (lag in ~375 frames of motion) | worst visible undrawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | 63 | 12 | baseline (2 on the leading edge, mid-frame) |
| pre (`463dcfe5`, no soft budget) | 67 | | |
| column cap 2 per tick | 51 | **19** | same |
| column cap 3 | 65 (core `5154203f`) | 14 | same |
| graded budget (2 decodes at lead 4-7 rows / 5-9 columns) | 62 | | |
| graded + column cap 2 | **49** | 11 | same |
| graded + column cap 3 | 57 | | |

- The gains are concentrated in one leg (the spindash dive, 25 -> 16-18), and per-leg results
  swing both ways between variants: lag here is chaotic in which tick tips over.
- **Column cap 2 equals the camera's 2 columns a tick**, so it has no catch-up headroom (rows
  already live with that, `VFILL_ROWS_PER_FRAME`'s note). It made max-diagonal flight worse
  (12 -> 19), and graded + cap 2's win rests on it. A cap of 3 has headroom and buys nothing.
- None moved the coverage instrument, so none drew a hole; none is a clear, correct change
  either. Not landed. The instrument that would make this tractable is per-tick work
  (below): lag counts alone cannot separate a better policy from a lucky one.

### What remains, priced

1. **Decode bursts (62% of lag frames)** are the demand fill catching up a row strip. The lever
   is speculation that lands at a diagonal: the prefetch is suppressed there by the residency
   guard (`BLOCK_SPEC_*`, 16 staging slots, measured 4.13 claims/tick at max diagonal). More
   staging slots cost 768 bytes each (`BLOCK_RAW_SIZE`); 16 more is 12 KB of RAM and the
   `rol.w` memo mask widened. Design-scale, not built.
2. **Column catch-up (27%)**: a starved pending column. A per-tick column cap needs headroom
   over the camera (so 3), which measured neutral; the real fix is not starving the column
   (split the soft allowance per axis, or let a pending column take the soft decode before
   a pending row). Not measured.
3. **Baseline**: RPC-1's faster plain copy loops (`PatchRun_Seq` 4.2k + `PatchRun_Col` 3.2k per
   diagonal tick today; the booked estimate is ~22 -> 15 and ~38 -> 20 cycles per word) are worth
   about 3k per tick. `TileCache_FillRow` self is 6.3k per row (12.5k a tick), mostly per-block
   setup and the byte-wise collision copy. Not built.
4. **The DEBUG shape**: 12 of 18 frames on the spawn legs. `Canopy_Probe` is the one named
   routine worth disarming (about 7 frames over 16 legs); that is the owner's call.
5. **Content**: sections 1-8 have no collision, so every exit from section 0 is a 5,300 px
   diagonal fall, which is the one motion this engine cannot yet afford. Authoring floors in
   those sections would also remove most of the lag he feels, and most of Part 2's fall.

## Part 2: the fall

### Our cap, S3K, S2, S.C.E.

| | fall cap (y_vel, air) | where |
|---|---|---|
| **Aeon** | **$F00 = 15 px/frame**, derived: one pixel under a 16 px collision cell | `engine/system/constants.emp:210` (`PHYS_FALL_CAP`), applied `games/sonic4/player/player_air.emp:261-264` (also `player_fly.emp:191`, `player_glide.emp:512`) |
| **S3K** | **none**: `MoveSprite` moves by the old y_vel and adds $38, no clamp, in or out of water | `skdisasm/sonic3k.asm:36035` (`MoveSprite`), from `Sonic_MdAir:22354` via `MoveSprite_TestGravity:36071`. Underwater only reduces gravity (`:22359-22361`). The $FC0 clamps at `:24112` / `:28948` are the steep-landing conversion, not a fall cap |
| **S2** | **none** | `s2disasm/s2.asm:36163` (`Obj01_MdAir`) -> `:29942` (`ObjectMoveAndFall`); `:37604` is the same steep-landing clamp |
| **S.C.E.** | $1000 = 16 px/frame | `Objects/Players/Sonic/Sonic.asm:435-437` (air), `:508-510` (jump) |

**Ours differs from S3K deliberately.** The owner ruled it on 2026-08-26 (`d-18-answered`, queue
row `FALL-CAP-15`, ARCH's "Fall cap — DERIVED" paragraph): the floor probe reads 16 px cells, so a
16 px step can tunnel through a one-cell floor. S3K gets an uncapped fall for free because its
collision is fully RAM-resident and camera-independent; ours streams with the camera. So this is
not fixed to match S3K here: that would reverse a ruling and reopen a known tunnelling hole.

### The camera's vertical rule

| | airborne window | focal point | step cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aeon** | +-32 px around the focal point, in the air AND on the ground | screen centre, **112** | **16 px/frame**, all states |
| **S3K** | +-$20 around the bias (`sonic3k.asm:38460`, `:38463`) | `Distance_from_top` **$60 = 96** (`:38088`) | **$1800 = 24 px/frame** airborne (`:38511`); ground: $600 (6) under ground speed $800, $1800 above (`:38491-38493`), $200 when the bias is moved (`:38502`) |
| **S2** | +-$20 (`s2.asm:18141`, `:18144`) | (224/2)-16 = **96** (`:14688`) | **16 px/frame** (`:18190`); ground 6 or 16 (`:18167-18170`), 2 when biased (`:18179`) |

Aeon: `engine/level/camera.emp:42` (`CAM_Y_DEADZONE`), `:350-420` (the follow and the cap),
`engine/system/constants.emp:571` (`CAM_SCREEN_HALF_H`), `:1146` (`CAM_MAX_Y_STEP`). The camera
runs after the objects in both engines (`ojz_scroll_test.emp`: `RunObjects`, then
`Camera_Update`), so neither lags a frame.

### Measured and modelled, frame by frame

**Measured on the ROM** (`fall_down` leg, straight fall from the top of the act): y_vel reaches
$F00 on the 6th recorded frame and holds it for 358 frames; the camera steps 15 px every frame,
the same as the player, so the camera never hits its 16 px cap. The player's sprite centre sits at
**screen line 144** throughout (focal 112 + deadzone 32). The probe reads 159 because its snapshot
lands between `RunObjects` and `Camera_Update` (the camera it reads is the previous tick's; checked
step by step: `cy` advances by exactly `py_prev - cy_prev - 144`).

**Modelled** (`fall_model.py`, each rule set's own arithmetic, cited in the tool):

| fall height | rule | frames | landing speed | lowest sprite line on screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 512 | Aeon / S.C.E. / S2 / S3K | 69 | 15.0-15.1 | Aeon 144, others 128 |
| 1,024 | Aeon | 104 | 15.0 | 144 |
| 1,024 | S3K | 98 | 21.4 | 128 |
| 2,048 | Aeon | 172 | 15.0 | 144 |
| 2,048 | S3K | 138 | 30.2 | 212 (the camera's 24 px cap loses him to the bottom edge) |
| **5,400 (section 0 to the act floor)** | **Aeon** | **395 (6.6 s)** | 15.0, at the cap for 327 frames | 144 |
| 5,400 | S.C.E. | 375 (6.3 s) | 16.0 | 128 |
| 5,400 | S3K | 223 (3.7 s) | 48.8 | off screen (1,519) |

- **Under 512 px the four rules are the same fall.** Every drop in S3K's own levels is in that
  range or a little past it, which is why S3K's missing cap never shows.
- **OJZ act 1's fall is 5,400 px.** Ours spends 327 frames (5.5 s) at a constant 15 px/frame; that
  is the "capped" he feels. S3K's rule would land in 3.7 s, but S3K's own camera (24 px cap) would
  lose Sonic off the bottom of the screen after about 2,000 px. No original rule set handles this
  fall well; S3K never had one.
- The camera is not what caps us: it keeps up (16 >= 15). The cap is the player's.
- Two differences from S3K in the camera, both a look rather than a speed: our focal point is
  16 px lower (Sonic falls at line 144, S3K 128, so he sees 16 px less below him), and on the
  ground our camera keeps the +-32 deadzone where S3K follows strictly.

### Options, priced, not built (the owner's call)

| option | what he would feel | cost and risk |
|---|---|---|
| A. Cap $1000 (S.C.E.) | 6.6 s -> 6.3 s; imperceptible | Reopens the 1 px tunnelling hole the ruling closed |
| B. Camera focal point 96 (S2/S3K) | Sees 16 px more below while falling; the whole game frames 16 px higher | One constant plus the warp and init centring (`center_camera_on`, `Camera_Init`); every screen changes |
| C. Fall look-ahead: when y_vel is at the cap, drift the focal point up 1 px a frame (camera 16 vs player 15) to e.g. 64 px | Long falls read as a dive, with 64 px more view below after ~1 s | ~20 lines in `Camera_Update`, within the 16 px camera cap and the streaming contract; changes the look only in long falls |
| D. Uncapped, S3K-faithful (`d-18-uncapped-option`, DEFERRED_WORK) | 3.7 s fall, accelerating | Needs sub-stepped vertical collision (a step over 15 px skips a one-cell floor), a camera over 16 px/frame (so `VFILL_ROWS_PER_FRAME` 3, about +10k cycles a tick in exactly the diagonal fall that already lags), and collision residency ahead of the camera. The camera would still lose him on a 5,400 px fall unless it is uncapped too |
| E. Content | Shorter falls | Floors in sections 1-8, which Part 1 also wants |

C is the cheapest option that changes the feel of this act's fall without touching the physics
ruling or the streaming budget. D is the faithful one and is a parcel plus a perf problem.

## Method notes

- **No emulator MCP.** Every run is the headless `oracle-aether` subprocess, sockets under
  `$HOME/ehzlag/sock`.
- **Emulator core.** The oracle and sigil `target/` directories vanished at about 17:53 local
  (21:54Z). The first leg runs (`legs/`, `legtable_stubs_core5154.txt`) used the running
  core recovered from `/proc/<pid>/exe` (sha1 `5154203f`); every comparison table except that one
  was re-run on the rebuilt core, **oracle-aether md5 `3e7c2778`** (oracle `549dc4f`), and base, pre
  and cap2 gave identical per-leg counts on both cores. During the outage I built sigil from the
  sigil checkout (read only, HEAD `7107ab15`) into a private target dir, before the controller
  asked agents not to; it reproduced the base ROM byte for byte (`8e6de1fa`). Every ROM named in
  the tables was then rebuilt with the restored shared sigil (md5 `b64c812d`), same CRCs.
- **Load.** Headless lag counts do not change with host load; loadavg ran 3 to 25 during the runs.
- **The probe's snapshot is mid-tick** (after `RunObjects`, before `Camera_Update`), so the camera
  it reads is one tick behind the player it reads. Lag counting is unaffected (it sums counters
  over frames); the Part 2 screen line is derived with that correction.
- **Picture identity**: `--dump` + `picture_cmp.py` hash, per on-time tick, the scroll buffers,
  VRAM Plane A over the camera window and all of Plane B. The timing-only control (base vs
  `Canopy_Probe` stubbed, slab spindash) is identical at 550 of 550 ticks. It was not needed for a
  landing, because nothing landed.
- **Per-tick work is not available per frame**: `set_profiler{perFrame:true}` returns whole-frame
  cycles including idle, and a one-frame window records nothing, so per-tick figures come from
  windows around lag frames.
