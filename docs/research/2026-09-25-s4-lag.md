# "s4 is now super laggy": where the lag is (2026-09-25)

Research parcel, no engine change. The owner said *"s4 is now super laggy"* (heard 13:12Z) after
flying the Sonic 2 two-zone act. He later confirmed, from a screenshot of his window, that the ROM
loaded at the time was **`s4.s2clip.debug.bin`**. This report measures, for that ROM and three
controls, (a) **game-side lag**: the 68000 main loop missing VBlank, and (b) **host-side lag**: the
emulator falling behind real time on this box.

## Verdict, one sentence per ROM

Figures are `lag frames / video frames` while the camera is moving. A lag frame is one VBlank that
ran `VInt_Lag` because the main loop had not reached `VSync_Wait` yet.

- **`s4.s2clip.debug.bin` (crc `441f1db0`, 848,912 B), the ROM he flew: game-side lag YES, and heavy
  where the level is painted.** Flying through the Emerald Hill band (camera y 0..1023):
  **30 / 86 frames going straight down (35%)** and **56 / 112 frames on the diagonal (50%)**. Whole
  legs: down 34 / 397, diagonal 117 / 987, right along y = 144 24 / 894 (2.7%), physics run
  243 / 2,706 (9.0%). **Host-side: keeps real time.** The headless core ran 376 to 402 fps at
  loadavg 4.7 to 8.4. `oracle-player --mode bench-cpu` on this ROM held 59.91 and 59.95 fps at
  loadavg 8.4 to 22.9.
- **`s4.s2clip.bin` (crc `9a3533f1`, 822,334 B): game-side lag YES.** Physics run 185 / 2,838 (6.5%),
  147 / 688 of those frames in camera y < 1024 (21%). One profiled window, running right from
  camera (407,506) to (894,541): **32 / 111 (29%)**. **Host-side: keeps real time**, 329 to 383 fps
  headless at loadavg 4.7 to 11.
- **`s4.debug.bin` (crc `62238a15`, 848,075 B), canonical control: game-side lag at the long-standing
  baseline only.** Right 6 / 364 (1.65%) and down 6 / 369 (1.63%), both made entirely of the
  DEBUG-only residency audit (2 lag frames every 128 ticks). Diagonal 49 / 412 (11.9%, the known
  max-diagonal cost), physics 66 / 1,179 (5.6%). **Host-side: keeps real time**, 313 to 386 fps
  headless.
- **`s4.bin` (crc `6d1af7a3`, 821,479 B), canonical release: game-side lag low.** Physics
  33 / 1,161 (2.8%). **Host-side: keeps real time**, 298 to 383 fps headless.

**Where the lag comes from.** It is game-side, it is specific to the two-zone clip act, and the
cause is the art-pool **page streaming path**, which is running on a flown ROM for the first time.
The two-zone act has **14 pool pages against `PAGE_FRAMES` = 12**, so it is not fully resident.
Every act flown before this one fit in the frames and took the fully-resident shortcut (canonical
OJZ: 10 pages; the previous clip act `s2_ehz_boot`: 8). Two costs show up:

1. `PageCache_Prefetch`'s `.scan_block` word walk runs in `VSync_Wait`'s idle slot and costs
   **23k to 94k cycles per tick** while the camera moves. It is not banked, so it runs past VBlank
   and delays the next tick. On a resident act it exits at its first instruction.
2. `PageCache_PatchRun_Seq` / `_Col` go through the page table instead of the direct map, which
   makes each call **about 1.9 to 2.8 times as expensive**.

The canonical ROMs have not changed bytes since at least 2026-09-18, so they are not the "now".

**Host-side: not found.** One thing stays open. The owner's own `oracle-player` main thread sits at
**95.9% of one core**. The same player binary family needs **3.3 to 3.4 ms of CPU per frame** in
`bench-cpu`, which does not explain that figure. The cause cannot be measured without touching his
window, so it is tagged for the controller at the end.

## Method and tools

**Tool choice.** Nothing in `tools/` drives a player through a whole act and counts lag frames per
camera position. `tick_variance_probe.py` / `engine_baseline_probe.py` measure fixed poke states and
use the old harness. `clip_reachability.py` is static. I wrote one small driver on the same
transport the gate harness uses: a fresh headless `oracle/target/release/oracle-aether <rom>
--no-pace` subprocess per leg, driven over the Aether bus by the same `Server` / `BusClient`
pattern as `tools/tick_variance_probe.py`. It never used an `mcp__oracle__*` tool, never touched
pid 2950, and ran one emulator at a time. The scripts are committed beside this report in
`docs/research/2026-09-25-s4-lag/`:

- `lag_flythrough_probe.py` resets and boots 300 frames. In `run` mode it presses B only when the
  player's y does not move over 8 frames. It then holds the named d-pad direction(s) and steps
  **one video frame per `run_frames(1)`**, reading `Frame_Counter` (+1 per VBlank on both VInt
  paths), `Logic_Tick` (+1 per completed tick), `Lag_Frame_Count` (DEBUG only, +1 in `VInt_Lag`),
  the camera and the leader's position.
  - Lag over any window = Σ dFrame_Counter − Σ dLogic_Tick. That is exact over a window but not
    per row: `run_frames` stops at a fixed cycle, so a late tick reads 0 then 2.
  - In every DEBUG leg it equals the `Lag_Frame_Count` delta exactly: 26 = 26, 245 = 245, 244 = 244,
    68 = 68 and so on.
  - The leg stops after the camera has been still for 60 frames (240 frames in run r1). "IN MOTION"
    figures drop that trailing rest.
  - Its host phase times one `run_frames(1800)` call while holding a direction.
- `lag_profile_window.py` does the same boot and drive, then takes one profiler sample on the new
  oracle's exact per-invocation profiler (the `tick_variance_probe` method: `run_frames(W+1)` holds
  W whole frames). It checks the server's completeness identity: the remainder is 0 in every sample
  here, with no truncation, abandoned frames or depth overflow. It prints cycles per logic tick.
- `residency_read.py` reads `PageIn_Fully_Resident`, `PageCache_Direct_Map` and `PageIn_Pool_Pages`
  after boot. `thread_cpu.py` gives per-thread CPU of a process from `/proc` (read-only).
  `run_legs.sh` and `summarise.py` drive the legs and print the tables. `lag_rows.py` lists where
  each lag frame fell.

**Motion, not rest.** Two kinds of leg:

- **fly** legs stay in DEBUG free flight. It moves at 16 px per *tick*, so lag slows the flight
  itself. The owner was sent to fly this act, so this is the likely owner condition.
- **run** legs leave free flight with B, hold right and tap C every 45 frames. In the release ROMs
  there is no free flight: the probe saw a standing player, and B was a jump. It is labelled
  "free flight detected" in the raw output. That label is wrong for release and harmless.

**Caveat on the physics legs.** On both clip ROMs, and on canonical, the physics run falls out of
the painted band. The camera ends at y ≈ 5,820 of the 6,144-px act, which matches the known
bottomless EHZ pits. The runner then covers most of its x-range in the empty void below. That is
why whole-leg percentages dilute. The camera-y buckets in the raw summaries separate the two.

**Frames covered, proven by leg count.**

| r2 (direction matrix) | 10 | 10 | `finished=10`, every rc=0 |

**Reading it.**

- On the streaming act the prefetch scan is the largest single row. It sits **inside `VSync_Wait`**
  (`PageIn_Process` → `PageCache_Prefetch`), so the "work" column cannot see it. But it runs past
  the VBlank it started before. `VSync_Wait` then returns immediately and the next tick starts
  late.
- In the release window the game's own work is only 0.57 frames per tick, and the scan alone adds
  0.74 frames.
- The scan's budget counts pages *requested*, not cycles. So when the ahead strip is all resident
  it walks every staged block's 256 words and finds nothing (`page_cache.emp` `.row_scan` /
  `.col_scan` / `.sb_loop`).
- On the diagonal the demand-first gate (`Cache_Art_Stall`) keeps the scan smaller (22.9k). The
  extra there is the page-table patch: PatchRun_Seq +23.1k and PatchRun_Col +11.3k per tick over
  the resident control. That is why stubbing prefetch did not help the diagonal.
- Even the resident control lags on the EHZ diagonal (19 / 61, work 1.06 frames per tick). Maximum
  free-flight diagonal is over budget on any act. That is the known max-diagonal cost
  (`docs/benchmarks/streaming/ARC-CLOSEOUT.md`: 1.192 frames/tick). Streaming raises it from about
  1.06 to 1.34 frames of work per tick, plus the scan.

**What the hypothesis forbids, and what was seen.** The hypothesis is that the clip-act lag is
caused by the streaming path (prefetch scan plus the non-direct patch) that only a non-resident
act takes. It forbids:

- (a) Prefetch cycles on the resident ROMs. **Seen:** 2,052 cycles over 54 calls on canonical,
  which is the early-out.
- (b) A resident clip act of the same zone lagging like the streaming one. **Seen:** the resident
  act lags like canonical (the eb table).
- (c) Removing the scan leaving the lag unchanged. **Seen:** it falls from 243 to 77 (physics) and
  from 34 to 16 (down), and fly-right falls to the audit pairs alone.
- (d) Heavy lag in the clip act's empty void rows. **Seen:** camera y ≥ 1024 shows only the audit
  cadence.

None of the four was contradicted. **Not fully explained:** in the stubbed ROM, flying down through
the EHZ band still lags 12 / 68 against 2 / 58 resident. Its work is 0.88 frames per tick, with the
page-table patch at 37.1k against 15.1k. I attribute that residue to the patch path and to demand
page-ins, but I did not decompose it further.

**How far localisation goes.** It reaches routine and label level (`PageCache_Prefetch.scan_block`,
`PageCache_PatchRun_Seq/_Col`) and act-level cause (14 pages > 12 frames). Within the act it is
**not** tied to the zone seam or to palettes:

- The heavy windows are at the *start* of Emerald Hill, camera x < 3,600, because that is where
  every vertical and diagonal leg crossed the painted band. The corridor and Chemical Plant were
  crossed only horizontally at y = 144 (plus in the void below), and there they show only the
  audit cadence. **Vertical motion inside Chemical Plant's painted rows was not measured.**
- `Parallax_Update` costs the same in every window: 7.7k to 8.8k per tick.
- No palette or region routine appears in any printed row set (top 30, or top 40 for the two
  diagonal windows).

## Not measured, and why

1. **The owner's window's real frame rate and pacing.** Getting it means querying pid 2950, for
   example its `emulator/pacing` over the Aether bus it serves (`--aether`). The brief forbids
   touching that process. **Tag for controller:** if a host-side answer for *his* window is wanted,

   read-only `emulator/pacing` call. The 95.9% main-thread figure is the open question.
2. **`bench-window`** (real window plus GPU present) was not run. It would open a window on his
   display.
3. **The exact commit where canonical bytes last changed.** The claim needed was "not
   last night", and that is proven. The date is bounded to ≤ 2026-09-18T18:59Z, not bisected.
4. **The stubbed-prefetch residue** in the EHZ band (12 / 68 against 2 / 58 resident) is not
   decomposed past "page-table patch plus demand path".
5. **Owner-like input.** No recording of his actual inputs exists. Free-flight right, down and
   diagonal plus a physics run bracket it, and his words ("we get to chemical plant") fit a flight
   across the whole act.

## Where this points (not a ruling)

The two-zone act is the first content the owner has flown on the streaming half of the paged art
pool. Every earlier flown act took the fully-resident shortcut (the residency table above). I did
not check which fixtures the streaming arc's own figures in ARC-CLOSEOUT were taken on.

What the measurements support, and how far:

- **Bounding the prefetch scan.** Give it a *cycle* bound, or make it bankable like the decoder, so
  it can never run past VBlank. The stub intervention shows this removes most of the excess when
  flying down (34 → 16) and running (243 → 77), and on the diagonal it removes almost nothing
  (56 → 52 in the band). That was measured, but with the scan deleted, not bounded.
- **Making the act resident.** Keep it at ≤ 12 pages, or give it more page frames. That would
  remove both costs, the scan and the page-table patch. This is **inferred** from the resident
  control act `76b237d3`. It was not measured on this act, and I did not measure what more frames
  would cost in VRAM or RAM.

Which one to take is the engine owner's call. The DEBUG audit pair every 128 ticks is a separate,
older, DEBUG-only hitch.
