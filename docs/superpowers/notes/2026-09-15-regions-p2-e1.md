The claim under test, verbatim from `engine/system/constants.emp:877-879`:

```
pub const PLANE_BUFFER_SIZE = 1536      // bytes (~11 FG column entries per frame; a BG
                                        // column entry is 132 B since the NEW-5 64-row
                                        // widening — worst realistic frame ~800 B)
```

Confirmed by the listing rather than trusted: `s4.debug.lst` puts `Plane_Buffer` at `$FFFFA746`
and `Plane_Buffer_Ptr` at `$FFFFAD46`, a gap of exactly `$600` = 1536. The probe asserts this
every run and refuses with exit 2 if source and ROM disagree.

### Entry sizes, read off the code that writes them

* **FG column entry** — `Draw_TileColumn` advances the cursor by `#4 + PLANE_V_CELLS*2` = **132 B**,
  plus `addq.w #4` when the column splits at the nametable row 63/0 seam = **136 B**
  (`engine/level/plane_buffer.emp`, `.sz_ok`). `Section_UpdateColumns` reserves the 136.
* **FG row entry** — `Draw_TileRow_FromCache`, header + `PLANE_H_CELLS` words = **132 B**.
* **BG column entry** — `Draw_BG_TileColumn` advances by `#4 + 64*2` = **132 B**. The comment's
  "a BG column entry is 132 B" is **correct**. See §5 for why no frame emits one.

### The streamer's own self-caps

Each of the four loops in `Section_UpdateStreaming` (`engine/level/section.emp`) refuses to start
an entry that would not fit:

```
cmpi.w  #PLANE_BUFFER_SIZE - 2 - (8 + PLANE_V_CELLS*2), Plane_Buffer_Ptr   ; > 1398 -> stop
cmpi.w  #PLANE_BUFFER_SIZE - 2 - (4 + PLANE_H_CELLS*2), Plane_Buffer_Ptr   ; > 1402 -> stop
```

So the pointer **cannot** pass the buffer, and the last admitted entry can carry it to 1534.
That is the ANALYTIC ceiling, and it is not the question: an overflow assert here would be a
guard that can never fire. The open question is occupancy, and only a latch answers it.

### Two deviations from the brief, both deliberate, both correctable if you disagree

1. **Not "beside `Plane_Buffer_Ptr`".** Every `@shape_divergent` group in `engine/ram.emp` sits at
   the RAM **tail**, and each one says in its own comment why: a DEBUG-shape addition there ripples
   **zero** existing engine-RAM addresses, moving only `Engine_RAM_End` and the game RAM chained
   after it. Beside `Plane_Buffer_Ptr` (~line 928) it would instead slide every engine-RAM field
   below it in the DEBUG shape — the one shape every gate and witness in `tools/` reads. The
   `Sprite_Owner` block records the identical deviation from its own booking for the identical
   reason. The cell is reached by absolute addressing from one proc; there is no locality to lose.
2. **A latch, not an assert.** See §1: an overflow assert would be vacuous.

### 536 is DERIVED, not just observed

```
CAM_MAX_X_STEP = CAM_MAX_Y_STEP = 16 px/frame;  a tile cell is 8 px
  => at most 16/8 = 2 new columns and 2 new rows per frame
column entry 136 B (split-at-seam worst case) · row entry 132 B
  => 2*136 + 2*132 = 272 + 264 = 536 B
```

The measurement matches the derivation **to the byte**, on six independent routes, and the
per-axis halves match separately (`right`/`left` = 272 = 2×136; `down`/`up` = 264 = 2×132). That
is the control on the enumeration: it says there is no producer in the frame that the derivation
missed.

### The stress routes came back empty, which is itself the finding

The routes `shake*`, `diag_long` and `warp_diag` exist to provoke the one thing the steady-state
derivation cannot bound: a **backlog**. Each streaming loop also stops when the tile-cache fill
left the next column half-written (`Cache_Fill_Resume_Col` / `Cache_Fill_RowResume_Row`), and a
loop blocked for k frames can advance k+2 columns in the frame the fill completes. **None of them
exceeded 536 B.** The tile-cache fill keeps up at the camera's cap, over 600 frames and across a
teleport. `shake2` reads 0 because reversing every 2 frames never leaves already-written ground —
a correct result, and a useful negative.

## 4. The decision table, derived

The thresholds below are computed from `PLANE_BUFFER_SIZE` and the row-entry size the code writes,
not copied. A wipe of N rows/frame costs `N * (4 + PLANE_H_CELLS*2)` = `N * 132` B. The binding
constraint is the row loop's own guard — an entry is admitted only while
`Plane_Buffer_Ptr <= PLANE_BUFFER_SIZE - 2 - 132` = **1402** — so beside a foreground peak P the
Nth wipe row must START at or below 1402: `P <= 1402 - (N-1)*132`.

| rows/frame | wipe cost | wipe duration (64 rows) | highest FG peak it still fits beside | at the measured 536 B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 132 B | 64 frames | 1402 B | fits, 868 B of the 1000 B headroom spare |
| 2 | 264 B | 32 frames | 1270 B | fits, 736 B spare |
| **4** | **528 B** | **16 frames** | **1006 B** | **fits, 472 B spare** |
| 7 | 924 B | 10 frames | 610 B | fits, 76 B spare — the most the guard admits |

(8 rows/frame does not fit: the 8th entry would start at 536 + 7×132 = 1460, above the 1402 guard,
and would be dropped silently.)

Against the brief's own table:

| peak | headroom | wipe rate | lands? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **<= 900 B** | **>= 600 B** | **4 rows/frame (528 B)** | **YES — measured 536 B / 1000 B** |
| 901-1200 B | 300-600 B | 2 rows/frame (264 B) | no |
| > 1200 B | < 300 B | 1 row/frame, or grow the buffer | no |

**ROW 1 LANDS.** Margin to row 2 **as the brief draws the line**: the peak would have to rise by
**364 B** (from 536 to 901) — a 68% increase — before the wipe drops to 2 rows/frame. Margin
**derived from the guard** rather than from the brief's rounder threshold: 4 rows/frame remains
affordable up to a peak of 1006 B, i.e. **470 B**. The brief's 900/600 split is the more
conservative of the two by ~106 B; the measurement clears both by a wide margin, so the two
derivations do not have to be reconciled to answer E1.

The third row does not land, so there is **no priced choice to put to you** between a 64-frame wipe
and 512 bytes of RAM. Neither is needed.

### Recommendation

```
const BG_WIPE_ROWS_PER_FRAME = 4    // 4 * (4 + PLANE_H_CELLS*2) = 528 B of the 1000 B the
                                    // foreground leaves. MEASURED 2026-09-15 (REGIONS-P2 E1):
                                    // the FG peaks at 536 B of PLANE_BUFFER_SIZE = 1536 across
                                    // 14 routes incl. both diagonals at the camera's 16 px/frame
                                    // cap on both axes, a 600-frame diagonal and a post-teleport
                                    // one. 536 is also the derivation: 2 columns * 136 + 2 rows
                                    // * 132. Re-measure with tools/plane_buffer_peak_probe.py if
                                    // CAM_MAX_*_STEP, PLANE_*_CELLS or the tile size ever move.
```

4 rows/frame is a **16-frame** wipe of the 64-row plane, which is exactly the figure the regrounding
study's design argument already assumes ("a 16-frame vertical wipe needs no cover",
`docs/research/2026-09-09-regions-study-reground.md` §4.3(e)). The measurement supports the design
that was already written rather than forcing it to change.

**ONE DESIGN CONSTRAINT THAT COMES WITH IT, and it is not optional.** The buffer is shared and
first-come. Whichever producer appends **second** is the one that gets clipped:

* If the **wipe appends first**, the FG streamer's guards cut the FG short — and
  `Draw_TileColumn` **drops a column and returns with no `raise_error` in every shape**, while
  `Section_UpdateColumns` records the column as written. The cost is a visible gap and a silent
  report (found by the 2026-09-08 probe, `d2c3dff0`).
* So the wipe must append **after** `Section_UpdateStreaming`, and it must carry its **own**
  reservation guard in the shape of `Draw_BG_TileColumn`'s (`plane_buffer.emp`, the `addi.w
  #4 + 64*2` overflow check). 528 B fitting inside 1000 B is a fact about the *budget*, not a
  substitute for the guard.

## 5. Verdict on the source comment's ~800 B claim

The comment at `engine/system/constants.emp:877` makes three claims. They do not all fare the same
and it would be wrong to grade them together.

* **"~11 FG column entries per frame" — CORRECT as a capacity statement.** 1536 / 136 = 11.3.
* **"a BG column entry is 132 B" — CORRECT.** `Draw_BG_TileColumn` advances by `#4 + 64*2`.
* **"worst realistic frame ~800 B" — NOT CONFIRMED. The measured worst realistic frame is 536 B,
  49% below it**, and 536 is not merely the worst observed: it is the mechanism's ceiling, derived
  from the camera's own step cap and matched to the byte. **No frame in the shipped ROM can reach
  800 B by foreground streaming.** The number is not merely unobserved, it is unreachable by the
  producer set that exists.

**A reconstruction of where 800 came from, offered as a hypothesis and flagged as one.**
536 + 2 × 132 = **800**, exactly. That is the same 2-columns-2-rows frame this experiment measured,
**plus two BG column entries** — which is what the same comment's middle clause is about. On that
reading the comment is not an error at all; it is a **projection of a frame that includes a
background producer**, and it happens to be arithmetically exact. I have not established that this
is what the author meant, and no commit message or design doc I found says so. Treat it as a fit,
not a finding.

**What makes the distinction load-bearing rather than pedantic:** `Draw_BG_TileColumn` has **zero
call sites in the tree.** A grep over every `.emp` and `.toml` returns only its own definition, two
prose lines in `engine/level/bg.emp`, and one plane-wrap `ensure`'s list of hand-spelled sites —
no `jbsr`, no `jbra`, no entry in any `map.toml`. **No frame in this ROM emits a BG column entry.**
So:

* Today's answer to "what does the foreground leave" is **1000 B**, not 736 B.
* The REGIONS-P2 wipe would be the buffer's **first** background producer.
* If the comment's 800 was indeed FG + 2 BG columns, then **it was already describing the world
  after this feature lands**, and a wipe at 4 rows/frame (528 B) puts the real steady-state total
  at 536 + 528 = **1064 B** — above the comment's 800, with the last wipe row starting at 932
  (470 B below the 1402 guard) and 472 B of the buffer still unused. **That is the number the next
  parcel should plan against, not 536 and not 800.**

**Recommended edit to the comment**, for whoever lands the wipe (not applied here — E1 is a
measurement parcel and changing the constant's prose is the wipe parcel's business):

```
pub const PLANE_BUFFER_SIZE = 1536      // bytes. MEASURED 2026-09-15 (REGIONS-P2 E1): the FG
                                        // streamer peaks at 536 B (2 col entries at 136 + 2 row
                                        // entries at 132 — the camera's 16 px/frame cap over an
                                        // 8 px cell), leaving 1000 B. A BG column entry is 132 B
                                        // and nothing emits one yet.
```

## 6. What I could not establish, named as such

1. **Whether a physics fall really cannot beat free flight.** The argument is that
   `PLAYER_DEBUG_FLY_SPEED = 16` is spelled "matched to `CAM_MAX_Y_STEP`" and `Camera_Update`
   clamps the Y step at `CAM_MAX_Y_STEP` regardless of player speed, so the camera cannot move
   faster than free flight however fast the player falls — and the streamer keys off the **camera**,
   not the player. I read both clamps and believe it, but I did **not** drive a real physics fall
   through a real shaft to confirm it empirically; the DEBUG shape boots into free flight and
   leaving it needs a B press and a landing. **TAGGED for your emulator follow-up if you want it
   closed by observation rather than by the clamp:** boot `s4.debug.bin`, press **B** to leave free
   flight, fall down a shaft, and read `Plane_Buffer_Peak` (`$FFFFEC52` in this build's
   `s4.debug.lst`) — zero it first. My prediction is **≤ 264 B**, strictly below the 536 the
   diagonal reaches, because a fall streams rows only. A reading above 536 refutes the derivation
   in §3 and the recommendation with it.
2. **Whether `Draw_BG_TileColumn` is linked at all.** I established it has no callers. I did not
   check whether the linker drops it or whether it is reachable through `map.toml` as dead weight,
   and `SIGIL_WARNINGS=full` would say. Irrelevant to E1's number; relevant to whoever writes the
   wipe, who should decide whether to build on it or replace it.
3. ~~The landing script was not run.~~ **It was.** `./tools/landing_build.sh` — **exit 0**, stamp
   `finished=0`, `EXIT_s4=0 EXIT_s4.debug=0 EXIT_demo.debug=0`, needs-build lane
   `EXIT_needs_build=0` with **13 marked tests RAN and 1 EXEMPTED** (`test_deb2_appendix[demo.bin]`,
   the one artifact its shape list does not build). And `s4.bin` came out of that build as
   `bd3eb49e…` a **third** time — baseline, post-latch, and landing — so the release-unmoved claim
