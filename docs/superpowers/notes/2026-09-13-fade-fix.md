## 3. (B1) — the fade ends when the colours arrive

**Proved from source first.** Once every channel equals its target: `step_d3_toward_d4` leaves an equal
channel alone and the word is rebuilt from its three channels, so a later step is the identity; and the
closing snap writes `Pal_Target`, which the buffer already equals in every channel bit. Words could differ
only in bits outside `$0EEE`, which CRAM cannot hold and the derive masks off, and the early close copies
`Pal_Target` verbatim anyway, so the end state is the full-length fade's, word for word. The only other
writers of the buffer during a fade are the cycle and operator layers (§1 rows 6 and 7). Neither co-occurs
with a fade in shipped content, and for both the early end is the better answer: the fade stops fighting them.

**Implemented:** the step loop ORs `(new ^ target)` into d6 and `(old ^ new)` into d5 (+16 cycles a word), and
`andi.w #$0EEE, d6 / beq .arrived` runs the close at once and clears the count. A step that arrives having
moved nothing (a re-arm toward the palette already showing: the DEBUG warp inside the cached night region)
closes WITHOUT publishing, so it costs no derive; the witness's finding W now reads `no fade in flight after
any compose`. The count reaching 0 at k = 16 is the backstop close for a buffer another layer keeps moving.
Capstone on the built ROM: `eor.w d2,d0 / or.w d0,d5 / eor.w d2,d1 / or.w d1,d6`, `andi.w #$eee,d6 / beq.b
.arrived`, then `.arrived: clr.b $8f0a.w / andi.w #$eee,d5 / bra.w .close`.

## 5. (B2) — is the re-derive per step necessary, and what it became

**It is necessary whenever the step moves colour AND a live raster program reads the slot.** The staging
image is what `OP_PAL_REGION` streams mid-frame and what a patched program's off-screen ship DMAs from
(`raster.emp`: the region op, and `Raster_BuildShipEntry`). A derive skipped on a moved frame would stream
the OLD palette's variant below the waterline. In the night region NO program reads it (`raster:
Raster_Program_None`), so there every derive is wasted, but the palette module cannot know that.

**Made cheaper, locally.** A channel holds one of 8 values, so the transform is precomputed per call into
three 8-word tables on the stack, each entry already in its channel's bit position, and each of the 48 words
becomes three indexed lookups: `(sp,d1.w)`, `16(sp,d1.w)`, `32(sp,d0.w)`, where the index is the channel's
bits masked with `$000E` (the channel times 2, which is a word offset). The output is identical bit for bit.
No RAM, struct or signature change; 48 bytes of stack during the call. `clamp_d1_07` lost its only caller
and is deleted (byte-neutral, measured). Capstone: `4fefffd0 lea -$30(a7),a7`, `38371000 move.w
(a7,d1.w),d4`, `88771010 or.w $10(a7,d1.w),d4`, `88770020 or.w $20(a7,d0.w),d4`, `4fef0030 lea $30(a7),a7`.
`tools/palette_variant_gate.py` on it: rc=0 (every vector, the 48-entry sweeps, `v_lines` coverage).
**18,946 -> 9,096 cycles.**

**Priced, NOT done: option P1, derive only what a live program reads.** The raster install would publish
which staging slots its program reads (a comptime slot mask from `raster_dsl`, or a scan of the program's
ops at install time); `Palette_Compose` would derive only those slots; installing a program that reads a slot
would mark it stale so that its first frame derives. RAM: 0 if the mask lives in `Pal_Active`'s free bits
6-7, otherwise 1 byte (a layout change that moves every downstream RAM pin). Code: roughly 40-60 bytes
across the two installers and the compose gate. Saves 9,096 cycles on every palette-moving frame in a region
whose program reads no slot (the night region, rows 1 and 2, `OJZ_Preset_Plain`, ...). What changes: the
palette module starts depending on what the raster tier consumes, a new cross-module contract. That is the
variant-system redesign the brief said to stop at.

**Priced, NOT done: option P2, a whole-word fade step.** The step's own ~17,400 (B1's accumulators included)
is now the larger half of a step frame. Guard bits at 4, 8 and 12 let one word's three channels step at once,
with no per-channel compare-and-branch: `GE = ((cur|$1110) - tgt) & $1110`, `LE = ((tgt|$1110) - cur) &
$1110`, `new = cur - ((GE & ~LE) >> 3) + ((LE & ~GE) >> 3)`, on `$0EEE`-masked words. Checked exhaustively
against the witness's `step_word()`: 262,147 (cur, target) pairs, stray-bit cases included, 0 mismatches.
Estimated ~150 cycles a word against the measured ~360, so ~7,000 a step frame against ~17,400, at about the
same code size; the witness's model is unchanged (same semantics). Not done: the brief scoped (B2) to the
derive, and P2 does not remove the lag frame (§6).

## 6. (B3) — the lag frame, attributed

outbound twin was measured as slack (the releasing VBlank minus the `VSync_Wait` entry, minus one period per

| build | slack of the k = 3 tick | the arm tick (starts at 4800) |

That tick is streaming-heavy: it has 2,576 cycles to spare with NO fade. A step that moves colour costs at
least a derive (9,096 after B2) plus a step (~17,400; ~7,000 with P2), so **no local cut removes this lag
frame**. B1 and B2 shrink the overrun by 9,052 cycles and do not remove it. Removing it needs the fade to
yield to a tight frame (defer a step when the frame's budget is short), which is a scheduling design
question, booked. The four other lag ticks sit near the section lines at 2048 and 4096 and are identical with
and without the fade.

## 7. (C) — the comments

`Palette_DoFade`'s header and inline comments now say what the branch does: it steps when the DECREMENTED
count is odd (k = 1, 3, ..., 15 after the arm, the first on the arming tick itself), returns on even counts,
and (since B1) closes at arrival. `publish_compose_lines()`'s note said "fade (odd-frame parity)" and now
says "it steps on odd counts only". The code's parity is unchanged: nothing depends on it outside the proc
(the witness transcribes it). Odd buys only the step on the arming tick; even would first move at k = 2 and
step 7 times, which still reaches d = 7. Comment-only: the dev build was byte-identical (`b3b05979` before
and after).
