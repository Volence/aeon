# The night palette's problems are ONE mechanism, and it is in the transform, not the colours

The hub ruled NIGHT-REGION-LOOK "tone down" on a Fable agent's read, with channel means measured
off the captures: the dirt block hue-shifts to wine-red rather than darkening (day mean RGB about
165/104/42, night about 91/29/39, saturation as high as the day colour); the canopy and trunk
bands collapse from mean value about 29 to about 14-17, near-black, merging pillars with the gaps
between them; the leafy terrain survives and reads as the same bush at night; and two elements sit
at what looks like full daylight.

**Those are not four art problems. They are four faces of one arithmetic error**, and it is
derivable from the source rather than from the pictures.

## The transform

`OJZ_Palette_Night` is generated by one recipe, written in `games/sonic4/data/effects/ojz_effects.emp`
above the array: every colour of `OJZ_Palette` with its 3-bit channels moved by **red -3, green -2,
blue 0**, each clamped to 0..7.

**Verified against the running machine, not taken from the comment.** CRAM lines 1-3 read out of a
booted `s4.debug.bin` inside the night region are bit-identical to `transform(ojz_palette.bin)` for
all 48 entries — **0 disagreements**. So nothing was skipped and nothing drifted; the array is
exactly what the recipe says.

*(My first run of this comparison reported 41 disagreements. That was my own indexing error — the
recipe consumes the file's first 48 words and installs them at CRAM 16..63, and I had compared
against the file's entries 16..63. Recorded because a 41/48 mismatch is exactly the shape of a real
finding, and I nearly had one.)*

## Why a constant subtraction is not a darkening

**Subtracting a constant per channel is a HUE ROTATION whose severity is inversely proportional to
each channel's starting value.** A darkening preserves ratios; a subtraction does not. And the
three constants here are different, so the rotation has a direction: blue is subtracted 0 and
therefore survives intact, green loses 2, red loses 3.

Measured over the act's 48 colours, as the fraction of luminance each keeps:

| | |
|---|---|
| **4 entries keep 0%** — crushed to pure black | `r2g2b0`, `r1g0b0`, `r0g1b0`, `r0g1b0` |
| bright warm entries keep **67-70%** | `r7g7b0`, `r7g0b6`, `r7g6b7`, `r7g7b7` |
| one entry keeps **100%**, bit-identical | entry 18, `r0g0b4` — a pure blue |

**A cast that keeps between 0% and 100% of an entry's luminance depending on its hue is not a
cast.** Every symptom follows from that one line:

- **The wine-red dirt.** Browns are high red, mid green, low blue. `r6g4b2` → `r3g2b2`: green
  halves, blue is untouched, so red and blue end up comparable and the hue swings off brown toward
  red-purple with its saturation intact. The three neighbours in that ramp do the same
  (`r4g2b1`→`r1g0b1`, `r5g3b1`→`r2g1b1`, `r7g5b3`→`r4g3b3`). The measured "saturation as high as
  the day colour" is the signature of a rotation rather than a dim.
- **The near-black canopy and trunks.** Those colours already had every channel at 3 or below, so
  subtracting 3 and 2 CLAMPS them to zero. Four act colours become literally `$0000`. Silhouettes
  do not darken, they cease to exist — which is the playability cost the ruling names.
- **The bush that works.** Greens are low red, high green, mid blue: the channel losing 3 was
  already small, the channel losing 2 has room, and blue is preserved. Ratios survive roughly
  intact, so it reads as the same bush after dark. **The one element everybody liked is the one the
  arithmetic happened not to rotate.**
- **The blue-dominant survivors.** Blue is subtracted 0, so any colour whose blue dominates comes
  through unchanged — entry 18 exactly, bit for bit.

## What this means for the fix

The ruling's direction (keep the blue shift, keep the bush, lift canopy and trunk out of black,
take the red out of the dirt, desaturate it) is **one change, not five**: replace the per-channel
subtraction with a **multiplicative scale plus an explicit blue lean**.

A scale preserves ratios, so it cannot rotate a hue and cannot crush a dark colour to black — a
half-brightness brown stays brown, and a `r2g2b0` becomes `r1g1b0` instead of `$0000`. The blue
lean then becomes a deliberate, tunable term rather than an accident of which channel had the
smallest subtrahend. That is a one-line edit to the recipe in `ojz_effects.emp` and a regenerated
array, and it is still `REVERSIBLE IN ONE WORD` the way the current note promises.

**A property worth asserting when it is written**, because it is exactly what the current recipe
violates and nothing would have caught it: *no act colour may go to `$0000` unless it was `$0000`,
and the luminance retained must lie in a narrow band across all 48 entries.* A transform whose
retention ranges 0% to 100% is not a night cast, and that is checkable without an eye.

## The straggler question, MEASURED — and my own hypothesis was wrong

An earlier revision of this note said the two full-daylight stragglers (the grass patch in
`t5-f272-settled.png`, the vine in `t5-f312-sec4096.png`) were **probably palette line 0**, the
shared character line, and therefore aurora's `PALETTE-LINE0` card rather than this ruling.

**That is refuted.** Measured, with the control established first:

| frame | decodes to night palette | to line 0 | UNACCOUNTED |
|---|---|---|---|
| a freshly captured live night-region frame (the control) | **100.0%** | 0.0% | **0.0%** |
| `t5-f272-settled.png` — the frame the ruling was measured from | 54.1% | **0.0%** | **45.9%** |

**The control is what makes this readable.** A frame captured just now, in the night region on
`s4.debug.bin`, decodes **100.0%** to `transform(ojz_palette.bin)` with nothing left over — so the
decode and the intensity ramp are right, and an unaccounted fraction means something about the
frame rather than about the method.

Two things follow, and only two:

1. **`t5-f272-settled.png` is not showing the settled night palette**, despite its name. Nearly
   half its pixels are colours that are in neither palette, concentrated in the dirt ramp — which
   is exactly where the ruling's channel means were taken.
2. **The stragglers are NOT line 0.** Line 0 accounts for **0.0%** of that frame. Had the grass
   patch been drawn in character colours it would have decoded as line 0 and it does not. So there
   is nothing here to route to aurora, and the `PALETTE-LINE0` hypothesis is dead.

**WHAT I AM NOT CLAIMING: the cause of the 45.9%.** Candidates are a mid-fade palette, VDP
shadow/highlight, or something else. I wrote a test that appeared to show all of it lying on
day→night fade curves — 100% explained, 0% residue — and **killed it on its own control: that test
accepts 145 of the 512 possible 3-bit colours, 28% of the colour space.** A test that accepts a
quarter of everything distinguishes nothing. The result was not implausible, it was *beautiful*,
which is why implausibility could not have caught it.

**What this means for anyone acting on the ruling:** the transform finding above stands, because it
is derived from the source recipe and from CRAM read off a booted machine and never reads a
capture. The *numeric* targets taken from these frames should be treated as acceptance checks on
the result, never as inputs to the recipe — and **the frames should be re-captured settled before
any colour detail is judged from them again.**
