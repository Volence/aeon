## Three corrections to the brief, made before anything was built

**(a) The palette snap is `ep_transition == 0`, NOT `pcfg_transition == 1`.** These are two
different fields on two different records, with **opposite polarity**, and building the fixture
on the named one would have produced a fade:

| field | record | where | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ep_transition` | `EffectsPreset` | `engine/effects/preset.emp:67`, u16 @ `$24` | **PALETTE.** 0 = SNAP, non-zero = cross-fade. `Effects_InstallPreset` does `tst.w EffectsPreset.ep_transition(a3)` and arms `Palette_ArmFade` only when non-zero (`engine/effects/preset.emp:394-395`). |
| `pcfg_transition` | `ParallaxConfig` | `engine/structs.emp:273`, u8 @ `$08` | **SCROLL OFFSETS.** 0 = smooth lerp, 1 = instant snap. Nothing to do with colour. |

`pcfg_transition` is also *inert* at this edge either way: neither preset across it binds a
parallax config, so both rows resolve through `Effects_ResolveParallax` to the same act default
and no parallax transition is started at all.

**(b) Rows 1 and 9 ARE adjacent at `OJZ_NIGHT_X0` = 3400, exactly as the brief said — and that
is the wrong edge to put the snap on.** Read at the base revision
(`act_descriptor.emp:613-625`), row 1 is `x 2048..3399` bound to `OJZ_Preset_Sec1` and row 9 is
`x 3400..4799` bound to `OJZ_Preset_Night`. `OJZ_Preset_Night` already differs from its
neighbours in **exactly two** fields — `ep_pal` and `ep_transition: 1` — so the E2 fixture the
brief describes *is* "row 9 with `transition:` taken off". But that **replaces the shipped fade
fixture rather than sitting beside it**, and it takes `tools/region_fade_witness.py`'s whole
premise with it: its **E0** requires *exactly one* region row in the table to arm the fade, and
the fade edge to have a neighbour binding a **different** `ep_pal`. Making 3400 a snap leaves
that witness with no subject.

So the snap goes on a **second** edge, in the same top row, in the same ROM: `x = 5600`. The
owner then sees **fade at 3400 and snap at 5600 over the same art on one held RIGHT**, which is
a better A/B than two ROMs could give, and the shipped fade fixture and its witness are intact.

**(c) "A full 48-entry swap" is a 48-entry palette REPLACEMENT of which 41 entries actually
change colour.** Measured on `s4.debug.bin`: `OJZ_Palette` vs `OJZ_Palette_Night` differ at
**41 of 48** CRAM entries (line 1: 14/16, line 2: 13/16, line 3: 14/16). The 7 that match are
`[0, 1, 16, 18, 24, 32, 33]` — six of them `$0000` (black clamps to black under the night
transform's `-3 red / -2 green / 0 blue`) and one `$0800` at entry 18. All 48 words are written;
41 of them land on a different colour. Nothing about the fixture changes — it is the *count* in
the brief that is off by seven, and a "48 of 48 changed" claim in a later report would be wrong.

## 2. Proof the two presets differ ONLY in `ep_pal`

Two independent proofs, one structural and one on the emitted bytes.

**Structural.** `OJZ_Preset_NightSnap` is `ojz_e2_plain(OJZ_Palette_Night)`. Every field that is
not `ep_pal` is fixed inside that one `comptime fn`, so the two sides of the edge cannot drift
apart without `ojz_effects.emp:2017-2020` moving.

**On the bytes** (`s4.debug.bin`, symbols from `s4.debug.lst`, script at
`.runlogs/e2_verify.py` — gitignored, reproduced below):

```
46-byte preset diff, OJZ_Preset_Sec2 ($01553C) vs OJZ_Preset_NightSnap ($01570C):
    differing offsets [1, 2, 3]          <- ep_pal @ $00..$03, and nothing else
    ep_pal          Sec2 = $024E9A (OJZ_Palette)   NightSnap = $01567E (OJZ_Palette_Night)
    ep_transition   Sec2 = 0                       NightSnap = 0           <- BOTH SNAP

46-byte preset diff, OJZ_Preset_Sec2 vs OJZ_Preset_Night (the shipped FADE, $0156DE):
    differing offsets [1, 2, 3, 37]      <- ep_pal, AND $25 = the low byte of ep_transition
```

preset and the day preset it meets differ in four bytes, all of them `ep_pal`.** The snap preset
and the shipped fade preset differ *only* in the transition word, so the two edges in the DEBUG
ROM install the identical 48 colours by two different mechanisms — which is exactly the

## 3. The release ROM — what moved, measured, and a CORRECTION

**`s4.bin` is NOT byte-identical to master's, and it cannot be.** The brief's "proven by hash"
is not an available standard for any parcel that adds a symbol, and `build.sh`'s own header says
so (`build.sh:1086-1095`, the 2026-09-04 correction): the release ROM carries the deb2 symbol
appendix past `EndOfRom`, so *symbol names* land in `s4.bin`.

What was measured — same worktree, same sigil pair, master's release built first and kept:

| | master `55651631` | this branch |
|---|---|---|
| `s4.bin` sha256 | `bd3eb49e…` | `e131e9a6…` |
| file size | 820 511 | 820 515 (**+4**) |
| `EndOfRom` | `$0BDA02` | `$0BDA02` (**unmoved**) |
| image `[0, EndOfRom)` | — | **byte-identical except 3 bytes** |
| deb2 appendix | 43 805 B, 940 symbols | 43 809 B, 941 symbols |

The three differing bytes in the image are **`$18E`, `$18F` and `$1A7`** — the Genesis header's
checksum word and the low byte of the ROM-end address at `$1A4..$1A7`, which grew by the 4 bytes
the appendix grew by. **Zero bytes of executable code or data moved.** The one new symbol is
`OJZ_Preset_NightSnap`, which in release is a zero-length `data` at `$014D6C` (immediately after
`OJZ_Preset_Night`, emitting nothing); it costs 4 appendix bytes rather than its 20-character
name because the deb2 format shares the `OJZ_Preset_Night` prefix, and the literal string does
not appear in the ROM at all (`s4.bin.count(b'OJZ_Preset_NightSnap') == 0`).

**No release behaviour can reach the fixture**: the release region table is the same ten rows
with row 2 at `4800..6143`, and nothing in it names the snap preset.

**If strict hash identity is required**, the only way to get it is to add no symbol — e.g. by
making `OJZ_Preset_Night` a 2-element array in DEBUG and binding the snap row at
`OJZ_Preset_Night + sizeof(EffectsPreset)`. That was considered and **not** taken: it buys four
header bytes at the cost of a fixture whose second record has no name, and label arithmetic in a
`Label` argument is exactly the comptime-poisoning shape EMP_PITFALLS §5 warns about.
**Owner's call.**

not at 3400. Crossing **x = 3400** rightwards gives the cross-fade over the same 41 colours;
crossing **x = 4800** rightwards gives a night → day **snap** that already ships on master.
Fade in at 3400, snap out at 4800, snap in at 5600 — three of the four cases on one held RIGHT.

**What to look at on the crossing frame and the 16 after:**

* **The background art does not change and is not meant to.** Only the colours do. The question

* **Sonic himself.** Palette line 0 is the character's and is **not** swapped — so on the snap

* **The rings.** Their gold is on line 1, so they dim with the world.
* **Any single frame that is neither palette.** There should be none. A snap is one tick.
* **The seam edge itself** — whether the change appears to sweep, tear, or land everywhere at

**The engine state that says which frame is which** (read at

## 7. What a headless capture CANNOT settle — stated plainly

**It cannot answer E2.** "Does it read as a glitch" is a judgement about a one-frame colour step
inside a scene that is scrolling at 16 px per frame, at 60 Hz, watched by an eye that is tracking
the motion. A contact sheet of stills does not produce that percept for anyone, and a viewer
comparing `k-01.png` with `k+00.png` side by side is doing something the player never does.

What the capture *can* settle:

* **exactly what changes**, colour by colour, and what does not (line 0, the art);
* **on which frame** it changes, and that it is one frame and not several;
* that no intermediate palette is displayed — i.e. that this really is a snap and not a short
  fade;
* that the two sides are the two authored palettes and nothing else.

Those are the facts the look judgement is made *against*. The judgement itself needs the ROM
running, in motion, and is the owner's. **The capture is evidence for the question; it is not
an answer to it.**

## 9. Reproducing the byte proofs

`.runlogs/` is gitignored, so the verification script is reproduced here rather than committed:
read symbols out of the `.lst`'s `NAME : ADDR C |` lines (the `(0) bank/addr :` lines are a
*different* namespace and do not answer `lookup_symbol`), read the region table with
`tools/region_table.py`, then diff `rom[addr:addr+46]` for the two presets and
`rom[pal:pal+96]` for the two palettes. All four numbers in §2 and §3 come from that.
